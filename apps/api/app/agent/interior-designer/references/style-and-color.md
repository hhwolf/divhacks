# Style, color and materials reference

## Styles

Use these as shared vocabulary. Most real homes mix two. Name the mix ("mid-century with Japandi restraint") rather than forcing one label.

| Style | Recognize it by | Typical materials and palette |
|---|---|---|
| Mid-century modern | Tapered legs, low profiles, organic curves, 1945 to 1969 roots | Walnut, teak, molded plywood, mustard, teal, olive |
| Scandinavian | Light woods, white walls, simple forms, lots of daylight | Ash, oak, wool, linen, white, pale gray, soft black |
| Japandi | Scandinavian function plus Japanese restraint, low furniture | Oak, bamboo, paper, stone, clay, muted earth tones |
| Minimalist | Few objects, hidden storage, clean planes | Plaster, pale wood, concrete, white and neutral |
| Industrial | Exposed structure, raw metal, warehouse roots | Brick, steel, reclaimed wood, leather, charcoal, rust |
| Traditional | Symmetry, classic forms, detailed millwork | Dark woods, damask, velvet, deep reds, navy, gold |
| Transitional | Traditional shapes, simplified details | Mixed woods, neutral upholstery, greige, soft blue |
| Modern farmhouse | Rustic wood, shiplap, black hardware | White oak, painted wood, iron, cream, black accents |
| Bohemian | Layered pattern, plants, collected objects | Rattan, jute, kilim rugs, terracotta, saturated jewel tones |
| Coastal | Airy, light, casual | Whitewashed wood, linen, rope, sand, sky blue, white |
| Art deco | Geometry, glamour, symmetry | Brass, lacquer, marble, velvet, emerald, black, gold |
| Cozy / cottagecore | Soft, warm, handmade feel | Quilts, painted wood, florals, cream, sage, dusty rose |

## Color

- 60-30-10: 60 percent dominant color (walls, large rug), 30 percent secondary (upholstery, curtains), 10 percent accent (pillows, art, a single chair)
- Undertones matter more than the color name. A warm white next to a cool white makes both look dirty. Check paint samples against the floor and the biggest fabric in the room
- Test paint in large swatches (at least 2 x 2 ft) on two walls and look at them in morning, afternoon and lamp light
- North-facing rooms get cool, even light: warmer colors balance it. South-facing rooms get warm light: cool or neutral colors hold up
- Light colors make walls recede, dark colors pull them in. A dark color on all four walls of a small room can make it feel cozy on purpose; half-dark usually just looks unfinished
- Ceiling one shade lighter than the walls, or the same color, feels taller than stark white
- Sheen: flat or matte on ceilings, eggshell on living areas, satin in kitchens and baths, semi-gloss on trim and doors

## Materials and texture

- Mix at least three textures in a room (a smooth one, a rough one, a soft one) so a neutral palette doesn't feel flat
- Limit wood tones to two or three, and repeat each at least twice
- Limit metal finishes to two
- Performance fabric (solution-dyed acrylic, polyester blends) for homes with pets and kids. Linen and velvet show wear and pet hair
- Wool rugs outlast synthetics and resist stains. Jute sheds and is rough underfoot

## Renters

- Removable options: peel-and-stick wallpaper, tension rods, plug-in sconces, adhesive hooks rated for the weight, large rugs over bad flooring
- Swap light fixtures and cabinet hardware only if you keep the originals to reinstall
- Big mirrors, lamps and textiles move with you. Spend there
