# Space planning numbers

Working rules designers use. Meters first because the app stores meters; feet and inches in parentheses for talking to US users. "Tight" is the least you can get away with; "comfy" is what to aim for when there's room.

## Walking room

| Where | Tight | Comfy |
|---|---|---|
| Main path through the room (door to bed, door to desk) | 0.75 m (2 ft 6 in) | 0.9 m (3 ft) |
| Side path (squeezing past a bed corner or chair) | 0.6 m (2 ft) | 0.75 m (2 ft 6 in) |
| In front of a door (app rule) | 0.9 m (3 ft) | 0.9 m + the door swing |
| Beside a bed you get in and out of (app rule) | 0.6 m (2 ft) | 0.75 m (2 ft 6 in) |
| In front of a dresser or wardrobe (app rule) | 0.75 m (2 ft 6 in) | 0.9 m (3 ft) |

## Bedroom

| Mattress | Size |
|---|---|
| Twin | 0.97 x 1.91 m (38 x 75 in) |
| Twin XL (most dorms) | 0.97 x 2.03 m (38 x 80 in) |
| Full | 1.37 x 1.91 m (54 x 75 in) |
| Queen | 1.52 x 2.03 m (60 x 80 in) |
| King | 1.93 x 2.03 m (76 x 80 in) |

- Bed frames add 5 to 15 cm each way. Always use the frame size from the catalog, not the mattress size
- From the bed, the person should be able to see the door without lying in a straight line with it. People sleep better when they can see who's coming in and aren't the first thing in the doorway
- Headboard against a solid wall, not under a window if avoidable (drafts, sun at 6 am, nowhere to lean). In a tiny room, under the window is fine if it's the only wall that works
- Twin or full beds can go against a wall on one long side to free up floor. Queens and bigger shared by two people need both sides open
- Nightstand top within 5 cm of the mattress top

## Desk

- Desk depth: 0.5 m minimum for a laptop, 0.6 m for a monitor
- Chair room behind the desk: 0.75 m tight, 0.9 m comfy (the app checks 0.75 m)
- Best spot: beside a window so daylight comes from the side. Facing the window gives glare and a bright background on video calls; back to the window puts glare on the screen
- Try not to put the chair with its back to the door. If there's no choice, say so plainly
- Needs an outlet within reach or a short extension cord along the wall, never across a path

## Living and shared spaces

- Sofa to coffee table: 35 to 45 cm (14 to 18 in)
- Seats for talking: within 2.5 to 3 m (8 to 10 ft) of each other
- TV distance: about 1.5 to 2.5 times the screen size. A 55 in TV sits 2.1 to 3.4 m away
- Dining table edge to wall: 0.9 m if nobody walks behind, 1.1 to 1.2 m if people pass behind seated diners
- Kitchen aisle: 1.05 m for one cook, 1.2 m for two

## Small rooms (under about 14 m² / 150 sq ft)

- Push the big pieces to the walls and keep one open rectangle in the middle. One big open spot feels bigger than three small gaps
- Line the path from the door up with the open spot, so walking in feels clear
- Tall and narrow beats short and wide for storage (a tall bookcase over a long low one)
- Furniture on legs makes the floor look bigger than furniture that sits flat
- A mirror next to or across from the window bounces light around
- Pieces that do two jobs: bed with drawers, fold-down desk, storage ottoman
- A yoga mat is 1.8 x 0.6 m; you need about 1.8 x 1.2 m to move on it without hitting anything

## Light

- Every room wants three kinds: one for the whole room (ceiling or floor lamp), one for tasks (desk lamp, reading lamp), one for mood (a small lamp or string lights)
- Warm white bulbs (labeled 2700K or 3000K on the box) for bedrooms and living rooms
- Plug-in lamps and stick-on lights are fine for renters
